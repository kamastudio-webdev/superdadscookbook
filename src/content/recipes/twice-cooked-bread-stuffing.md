title: Twice-Cooked Bread Stuffing
description: A big pan of Thanksgiving bread stuffing that's cooked twice. Cubed white bread, celery and onions are browned in butter on the stove, then baked until crisp on top. George Washington's Golden Seasoning flavors the whole batch, and you can make it the night before.
category: Sides
prepMinutes: 30
cookMinutes: 50
servings: 5 to 6 people
date: 2026-11-26
tags: [stuffing, thanksgiving, holiday, make-ahead]
adaptedFrom:
  name: ManMadeDIY
  url: https://www.manmadediy.com/best-turkey-stuffing-recipe-ever/
ingredients:
  - 3 loaves white bread, cut into ¾- to 1-inch cubes
  - 12 stalks celery, chopped
  - 3 large onions, chopped
  - 1 small package gizzards, finely chopped (optional)
  - 2 tablespoons butter for the vegetables, plus more as needed
  - 1 stick butter, or more as needed, for browning the stuffing
  - 6 packets George Washington's Golden Seasoning and Broth
  - 2 cups warm water
steps:
  - Cut the bread into ¾- to 1-inch cubes and put them in your biggest bowl (or two bowls). You can do this a day ahead. Keep the cubes in a bag or a covered bowl.
  - Set two large nonstick skillets over medium-high heat and melt 1 tablespoon of butter in each. Cook the onions in one pan and the celery in the other, adding a little more butter as they cook, until they're soft and starting to brown at the edges. If you're using gizzards, cook them in the pan with the onions.
  - Add the onions and celery (and gizzards) to the bread and toss well.
  - Stir the seasoning packets into the 2 cups of warm water until they dissolve.
  - Pour the seasoned water over the bread a little at a time, tossing as you go. Stop when the bread is moist and clumps together when you squeeze a handful.
  - Put the two skillets back over medium-high heat with a generous piece of butter in each. Add the stuffing, working in batches if it doesn't all fit, and cook, turning it now and then, until the outside browns and dries a little. Add more butter whenever the pan looks dry.
  - "To serve it now: heat the oven to 350°F to 400°F. Pile the stuffing into a large glass baking dish and bake it uncovered for 15 to 20 minutes, until the top is browned and crisp."
  - "To make it ahead: pile the browned stuffing into the baking dish, cover it and refrigerate overnight. Take it out of the fridge about an hour before baking. Bake it covered at 350°F to 400°F for 45 minutes or more, until hot all the way through, then uncover it for the last few minutes so the top crisps."
tips:
  - Cooking the stuffing twice is the point of this recipe. Browning it in butter on the stove gives it crisp, buttery edges, and the oven finishes it with a crunchy top while the inside stays soft.
  - Any oven temperature from 350°F to 400°F works, so the stuffing can go in alongside the turkey or whatever else is baking.
  - The original recipe just says "lots of butter" for browning the stuffing. A stick is a starting point for 3 loaves; use more if the pan looks dry.
  - George Washington's Golden Seasoning and Broth comes in small packets, usually in a box near the bouillon. The original calls for 1 box or 6 packets. If you can't find it, chicken bouillon dissolved in the warm water will work, though the flavor will be different.
  - The gizzards are optional, but the original recipe recommends them. Chop them finely so they cook through with the onions.
  - Serve it with gravy. For 10 to 12 people, use the 2× button and make a double batch.
