---
title: Hamburger Buns
description: Soft homemade burger buns kneaded in the stand mixer, brushed with egg wash and baked golden. Makes 8.
category: Breads
prepMinutes: 30
cookMinutes: 17
restMinutes: 180
restLabel: Rise
servings: About 6 buns (130 g each)
date: 2026-11-07
tags: [baking, stand-mixer, cookout]
ingredients:
  - section: Dough
    items:
      - 2½ teaspoons yeast
      - 1 pound all-purpose flour (about 3½ cups), plus a little more for kneading and shaping
      - 1 cup warm water
      - 1 egg
      - 3 tablespoons butter, melted
      - 3 tablespoons sugar
      - 1¼ teaspoons salt
      - 1 teaspoon oil, for the bowl
  - section: Egg wash
    items:
      - 1 egg, beaten
      - 1 tablespoon milk
steps:
  - In the bowl of the stand mixer, whisk the yeast with ½ cup of the flour and the warm water. Let it stand for 10 to 15 minutes, until it looks bubbly.
  - Whisk the egg, melted butter, sugar and salt into the yeast mixture, then add the rest of the flour.
  - Put the dough hook on the mixer and knead on low (speed 3) for 6 minutes, scraping down the sides when needed. If the dough sticks to a spatula, add a little more flour.
  - Turn the dough out and shape it into a ball. Pour the oil into the mixer bowl, put the dough back in and turn it so it's coated all over. Cover the bowl and let the dough rise for 2 hours.
  - Turn the dough out onto a floured counter and pat it down to flatten the air bubbles. Divide it into 130 g pieces with a kitchen scale. You'll get about 6.
  - Pat each piece into a flat disc about ½ inch thick. Set them on a baking sheet about ½ inch apart and dust the tops lightly with flour.
  - Drape a sheet of plastic wrap loosely over the buns without tucking in the edges, and let them rise for 1 hour. Heat the oven to 375°F near the end of the rise.
  - Whisk the egg with the milk and brush it over the tops of the buns.
  - Bake for 15 to 17 minutes, until the buns are golden brown. Let them cool on a rack before slicing.
tips:
  - No scale? Cut the dough into 6 equal pieces. For smaller buns, make 8.
  - A ¼-ounce packet of yeast holds 2¼ teaspoons. Matt uses a little more, 2½ teaspoons.
