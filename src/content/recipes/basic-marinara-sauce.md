---
title: Basic Marinara Sauce
description: An old-fashioned, slow-simmered tomato sauce from The James Beard Cookbook, built on bacon, onions and garlic with a few anchovies for depth.
category: Basics
cuisine: Italian
prepMinutes: 20
cookMinutes: 120
servings: About 4 cups of sauce
date: 2026-11-08
tags: [sauce, pasta, tomato, family-recipe]
ingredients:
  - 6 slices bacon
  - ¼ cup olive oil
  - 3 onions, peeled and chopped
  - 3 cloves garlic, peeled and chopped
  - 3 lb fresh tomatoes, or 2 cans (No. 2½ size, about 28 oz each) solid-pack tomatoes
  - 3 anchovies
  - Fresh or dried basil, to taste
  - 3 sprigs parsley
  - Salt
  - Pepper
steps:
  - Cut the bacon into small pieces. Cook it in a large, heavy pot over medium heat until it's almost crisp.
  - Add the olive oil, onions and garlic. Cook, stirring often, until the onions are soft and nicely golden, about 10 minutes.
  - Add the tomatoes (fresh ones can go in unpeeled and with the seeds, just cut into chunks), along with the anchovies, basil, parsley, salt and pepper.
  - Turn the heat down and let it cook slowly, stirring now and then, until the tomatoes have broken down and the sauce has cooked down and thickened.
  - Pour the sauce through a strainer or food mill into a bowl, pressing on the solids to push through as much as you can. This takes out the skins, seeds and parsley stems.
  - Return the strained sauce to the pot and let it simmer gently for 1 more hour, stirring once in a while. Taste and add more salt and pepper if it needs it.
tips:
  - You don't need to peel or seed fresh tomatoes. The strainer catches the skins and seeds at the end.
  - The anchovies melt away and add a savory depth. You won't taste fish.
