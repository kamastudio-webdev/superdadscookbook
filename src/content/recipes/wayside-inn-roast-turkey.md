title: Wayside Inn Roast Turkey with Bread Stuffing and Giblet Gravy
description: The old New England holiday turkey from Longfellow's Wayside Inn in Sudbury, Massachusetts, filled with a ham, chicken and homemade sausage bread stuffing and served with giblet gravy.
category: Dinner
prepMinutes: 60
cookMinutes: 250
servings: 10 to 12 servings
date: 2026-11-08
tags: [turkey, thanksgiving, holiday, stuffing]
adaptedFrom:
  name: A Treasury of Great Recipes (Mary and Vincent Price), via Cast Iron Chaos
  url: https://modemac.com/roast-turkey-wayside-inn/
ingredients:
  - section: Turkey
    items:
      - 1 ready-to-cook turkey, about 12 pounds, with the giblets and neck
      - 2 teaspoons salt, divided
      - ¼ teaspoon pepper, plus a little more for the skin
      - ½ cup butter, softened
      - 2 slices bacon
  - section: For the roasting pan
    items:
      - 2 cups water
      - 1 onion
      - 1 stalk celery
      - 2 carrots
      - 2 bay leaves
      - 5 sprigs parsley
      - ½ teaspoon thyme
  - section: Giblet gravy
    items:
      - The turkey giblets, neck and wing tips
      - 1 quart water
      - 6 tablespoons fat from the roasting pan
      - 6 tablespoons flour
      - 3 cups liquid (the giblet broth plus the skimmed pan juices)
  - section: Wayside country sausage
    items:
      - 1 lb ground pork
      - 1 teaspoon salt
      - ½ teaspoon pepper
      - 1 teaspoon thyme
      - ¼ teaspoon dry mustard
      - 1 teaspoon crumbled sage
  - section: Wayside bread stuffing
    items:
      - ½ cup butter
      - 2 onions, chopped
      - 2 stalks celery, chopped
      - 1 cup finely diced cooked ham
      - 1 cup finely diced cooked chicken
      - ½ cup Wayside country sausage (above)
      - 6 cups day-old bread crumbs
      - 4 eggs, lightly beaten
      - 1 teaspoon crumbled sage
      - ¼ teaspoon pepper
      - ¼ teaspoon thyme
      - ¼ teaspoon mace
      - ¼ teaspoon marjoram
      - 1 teaspoon salt
      - ½ cup chicken stock
steps:
  - "Make the sausage: mix the ground pork with the salt, pepper, thyme, dry mustard and sage until the seasonings are spread evenly. Set aside ½ cup for the stuffing."
  - "Start the stuffing: melt the ½ cup of butter in a large skillet, add the onions and celery and cook over low heat for 10 minutes, until soft. Add the ham, chicken and the ½ cup of sausage, crumbling it in, and cook 5 minutes more, stirring now and then, until the sausage is no longer pink."
  - Put the bread crumbs in a large mixing bowl and pour the skillet mixture over them. Add the eggs, sage, pepper, thyme, mace, marjoram and salt and toss lightly. Stir in the chicken stock, just enough to moisten everything.
  - Heat the oven to 325°F. Take the giblets and neck out of the turkey and cut off the wing tips; save them all for the gravy. Rub the inside of the bird with 1 teaspoon of the salt and the ¼ teaspoon of pepper.
  - Spoon the stuffing loosely into the body and neck cavities. Tie the legs and wings snugly against the body.
  - Rub the skin all over with the softened butter and sprinkle it with the other teaspoon of salt and a little pepper. Set the turkey breast-side up in a shallow roasting pan and lay the bacon across the breast.
  - Pour the water into the pan and add the onion, celery and carrots (cut into chunks), bay leaves, parsley and thyme.
  - Roast for 3½ to 4 hours (10 to 20 minutes per pound), spooning the pan drippings over the turkey every 30 minutes, until the skin is an even, deep brown and a thermometer reads 165°F in the thickest part of the thigh and in the center of the stuffing.
  - "While the turkey roasts, make the giblet broth: simmer the giblets, neck and wing tips in the quart of water for 2 hours. Strain the broth and finely chop the giblets."
  - When the turkey is done, move it to a large warm platter. Pour the liquid from the roasting pan into a bowl and let the fat rise to the top.
  - Spoon 6 tablespoons of that fat back into the roasting pan and set it on the stove over medium heat. Stir in the flour and cook for 5 minutes, scraping up all the browned bits from the bottom and sides.
  - Skim the rest of the fat off the pan juices. Stir 3 cups of giblet broth and pan juices into the roasting pan and cook, stirring, until the gravy is thick and smooth. Add the chopped giblets, taste for salt and serve with the turkey.
tips:
  - Stuff the turkey right before it goes into the oven, never the night before.
  - The sausage recipe makes more than the stuffing needs. Shape the rest into patties and fry them for breakfast.
