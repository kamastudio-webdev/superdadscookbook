title: Construction Gingerbread House
description: A sturdy gingerbread that holds its shape in the oven, made for building houses, plus a stiff royal icing to glue the walls together. One batch makes 3 houses.
category: Dessert
prepMinutes: 30
cookMinutes: 60
restMinutes: 20
restLabel: Chill
servings: 3 gingerbread houses
date: 2026-11-07
tags: [christmas, holiday, baking, kids-project]
adaptedFrom:
  name: Sugar Geek Show
  url: https://sugargeekshow.com/gingerbread-house-recipe/
ingredients:
  - section: Gingerbread
    items:
      - 28 oz all-purpose flour (1¾ lb, about 6¼ cups)
      - ¾ teaspoon ground cinnamon
      - ¼ teaspoon ground ginger
      - ½ teaspoon ground nutmeg
      - ¼ teaspoon ground cloves
      - ¾ teaspoon salt
      - 7 oz vegetable shortening (about 1 cup)
      - 6 oz granulated sugar (about ¾ cup plus 2 tablespoons)
      - 16 oz molasses, by weight (about 1⅓ cups)
      - 1 large egg
      - 5 Jolly Ranchers, crushed, or isomalt, for the windows (optional)
  - section: Stiff royal icing
    items:
      - 16 oz powdered sugar (1 lb, about 4 cups), sifted
      - 2 oz pasteurized egg whites from a carton (about ¼ cup)
      - ¼ teaspoon cream of tartar
      - 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
steps:
  - Sift the flour, cinnamon, ginger, nutmeg, cloves and salt together into a bowl and set it aside.
  - Melt the shortening in the microwave or in a small pot on the stove until it's just liquid. It should feel warm, not hot.
  - In the stand mixer with the whisk, beat the melted shortening, sugar and molasses together. Add the egg and mix until it disappears into the mixture.
  - Switch to the paddle and add the dry ingredients. Mix on medium-low just until the dough starts to come together in a smooth ball. Stop there, because overmixing makes it tough.
  - Roll the dough out on parchment paper or a silicone baking mat to an even ¼ inch thick. Keeping the thickness the same all over helps every piece bake the same.
  - If you have time, slide the rolled dough onto a baking sheet and freeze it for 20 minutes. Cold dough cuts much cleaner.
  - Heat the oven to 300°F. Cut out the walls and roof with your house templates, leaving the pieces on the parchment, and peel away the extra dough around them. Press the scraps together and re-roll them for more pieces.
  - Slide the parchment onto a baking sheet and bake for 50 to 60 minutes, until the pieces are very firm. For candy windows, scatter the crushed candy into the window holes for the last 5 minutes of baking so it melts into clear panes.
  - Let the pieces cool completely on the pan before you move them. Warm gingerbread bends and cracks, and cool gingerbread is ready to build with.
  - For the icing, put the egg whites, sifted powdered sugar and cream of tartar in the stand mixer bowl with the whisk attached.
  - Mix on low until the sugar is wet, then turn the mixer up to high for 1 to 2 minutes. Add the vanilla and keep whipping until the icing turns bright white. It doesn't need more than 5 minutes in all.
  - Scoop the icing into a container with a tight lid right away so it doesn't crust over. Use it thick to glue the walls and roof together, or stir in a few drops of water at a time to thin it for piping details.
tips:
  - You'll need a gingerbread house template for the walls and roof. The source page has one you can print.
  - Matt's printed copy was scaled up to 8 houses, which is 2⅔ times everything here (about 4⅔ pounds of flour and 2⅔ eggs). That's more dough than a home stand mixer can handle, so make separate batches instead. Three batches make 9 houses.
  - The icing isn't cooked, so use pasteurized egg whites from a carton.
